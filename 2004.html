North County TimesMarch 21, 2004
Carlsbad peace rally draws mixed reactions

By: LOUISE CANNON - Staff Writer
CARLSBAD ---- Car horns blared, people shouted, dozens of thumbs up stuck out of car windows, and a few middle fingers waved Saturday afternoon as more than 100 peace demonstrators displayed a myriad of anti-war and anti-Bush signs along Carlsbad Village Drive. The rally, organized by the North County Coalition for Peace and Justice, coincided with hundreds of similar demonstrations worldwide to voice opposition to the U.S. presence and war in Iraq on the one-year anniversary of the invasion. "I did this last year when the war started. This is a bad war, like all wars, and it was based on lies," said Eric Parish, a Vista man holding up a poster-size peace sign and another that read "SHAME." The difference between Saturday's protest and the one last year is that weapons of mass destruction have yet to be found in Iraq, said Paul Motta, another member of the North County Coalition for Peace and Justice. It was President Bush's main reason for war, he said. "There are demonstrations like this all over the world," Motta said, waving an oversize American flag with a peace sign in place of the stars. "Everybody knows it was all lies. We know there were never any weapons of mass destruction. We knew it was all a lie." While the rally served to promote peace, it wasn't void of disputes in a town sprinkled with servicemen and -women just several miles south of Camp Pendleton. One protester, who did not give his name, was confronted by a man wearing a Marine Corps sweat shirt who demanded, using profanities, that the protester put away his anti-war sign. The protester refused and the other man threatened him with hot coffee in his hands. "I'm out here for you, for people like you," said the protester, as the man walked away shouting. Other confrontations were less harsh. The talk of lies among protesters is what made Marine Cpl. Shane Hazel, a 24-year-old war veteran who was walking through Carlsbad Village with a friend, stick around to talk to those holding signs. "I think a lot of people are misinformed," he said. "The signs are wrong. I'm totally for them doing this. It's their right. I'd like to ask them questions and see what they know." Hazel, who was in Iraq last year, spent several minutes talking to Iris and Ben Platnik, a couple from Carmel Valley who said they have been against the war from the beginning. "I like the idea that the lies are all unraveling," Iris said, which prompted several questions from Hazel. Encinitas resident Donna Taylor brought her sign to the rally for servicemen like Hazel. It was a less political and more factual: "573 American troops killed as of March 20." She said the number needed an update after Saturday's news reports. "It's changing as we speak, by the day," Taylor said. "These are all kids who joined the miliary for careers or benefits. All the people who've been killed ---- that's what brings me here. All of them (in Iraq) now." Not everybody appreciated Taylor's sign. Some drivers shouted, "You are all wrong." One person walking by rolled his eyes. To Carlsbad resident Daniel Teta, who was riding his beach cruiser near the rally with his daughter and friend, the protesters have guts. "I think this is great. It's what (troops) are fighting for, right? Freedom," he said. "I think it's pretty brazen of them to do it so close to a military town. Hats off to them." Facts of mosque raid missing

The lead story in the Nov. 20 North County Times was "Troops raid large mosque in Baghdad." The actual facts were missing, however.

During Friday prayers, American troops and Iraqi National Guard troops sealed off the Abu Hanifa mosque in Baghdad in order to detain the iman, Shaikh Muayid al-Adhami. There were 1,500 men, women and children inside praying at the time. Shots rang out and four worshippers were killed and at least nine wounded. Chaos ensued.
