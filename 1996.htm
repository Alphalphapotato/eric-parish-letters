Oceanside North County Times  January 6, 1996
Tired of arguing with born-again Christians
Editor:
 Let me see if I can explain this. This is for Mason Weaver mainly, but also for all you others who seem to think the letters section is a place to argue the lineage of Joseph and Mary. I personally do not care one bit whether or not you are a Christian and actually believe that a dead man walked out of his tomb after three days. You can believe anything you want- this is America. The thing about Christians is this idea that they and only they will go to heaven when they die. They are somehow "saved" vs. everyone else in the world being "damned." This causes an awful lot of prejudice and hate.
 If someone is damned, then you really don't care what they say or believe. You evangelical right-wing Christians lose track of the fact that we are all human beings. This idea of being "saved" also makes you people incredibly smug as only you and people who think exactly as you do will be the ones to survive after death. Not to mention the fact of your in-your-face insistence that everyone else has to think just as you do.
 So most of us unbelievers just keep smiling and let you guys go ahead and define a human fetus as a "preborn innocent human child" while a starving 1-year-old becomes a "welfare bum."
 Anyway, I am through. Arguing with a born-again Christian is one of the most useless activities I can think of anyway.
                                                                               ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  February 15, 1996
Columnist Downs out of touch with reality
Editor:
 I have been reading the columns by Jim Downs, our local "retired teacher," for what seems like centuries. Mostly, he sounds like any other old person, basically out of touch with reality in general.
 I was reminded of this by his Feb. 8 column, "Region cares for too many homeless." It is interesting that he thinks "North County is a destination stop for vagrants."
 We are also a "destination stop" for the old and tired affluent who think people not of their income level should move to the ghetto somewhere else.
 I really am sorry that Mr. Downs has to hurt his eyes looking at people pushing shopping carts on the streets of Oceanside. It must be very annoying for him.
 I don't know about his idea that Brother Benno's should quit being so generous with handing out food to the homeless families in our town. Maybe he should tout his idea next Christmas, maybe call it "Starve a homeless family for the holidays."
Downs was absolutely right about one thing, however. His ideas do sound very "mean-spirited."
                                                                                         ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  March 13, 1996
Letter showed ignorance of those on right
Editor:
 First off, I would like to say that I really like the work of columnist Kit-Bacon Gressitt. You need someone to balance the bile coming from the pen of columnist Mason Weaver.
 In the March 3 Letters section was a letter from Marcia Thomas trying to attack Gressitt's liberal reaction to life. I haven't laughed this hard in a long time. Poor Ms. Thomas must have skipped high school English lit. Walt Whitman was one of America's greatest poets, not a "Quasimodo-like lecher who drooled lasciviously over his students as he read his poetry." I read "Leaves of Grass" in my younger poetry reading days. It is hard to think of anything he wrote as being "pornographic." He wrote of life. Maybe you think life itself is "pornographic" and "filthy."
 The hilarious part was how you somehow were thinking he was physically there teaching Gressitt in her younger days. That would make Gressitt about 150 years old. Needless to say, I couldn't quite understand your point about liberals and the "rocks in the road of life." All I understood from your letter is the ignorance of many on the right who never let facts get in the way of their beliefs.
                                                                               ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  June 29, 1996
Editorial cartoonist shows agenda
Editor:
 Your cartoonist, Mark Thornhill, has again shown his agenda in referring to the justices who threw out the "three-strikes" law as "liberal judges" giving the finger to the public (June 23). It doesn't matter to Mr. Thornhill that six of the seven justices on the California Supreme Court were appointed by Republican governors.
 These are conservative justices who made this ruling. Rose Bird is long gone, Mr. Thornhill. I guess it doesn't matter that many of these "three strikes" that sent people to prison for life were for nonviolent crimes such as stealing pizza and shoplifting. Or that judges can still sentence third-time violent offenders to life without parole, if they think the case merits such a sentence. The whole ruling was about returning sentencing powers back to the judges who actually try the cases.
 Mr. Thornhill doesn't really care about things such as "facts" or "truth." He just likes bashing liberals and spewing hate- only when he's not parading his "Christianity" to us, of course.
                                                                                   ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  August 3, 1996
God's love is not selective
Editor:
 It is too bad your readers missed the message Kit-Bacon Gressitt was trying to make in her recent column about the rumor of her being a lesbian.
 I thought she made her point rather well. It shouldn't make any difference at all what one's sexual orientation is. The fact that some readers felt it was "gross" or had their religious sensibilities offended would seem to be their own problem, not Ms. Gressitt's.
 I refuse to believe that to be a Christian one must be disgusted by homosexuality. Confusing your own prejudice with the idea that gay people are an affront to God is wishful thinking. I seriously doubt that God much cares one way or another about with whom or where we humans use our sexual organs, and he sure didn't charge all who believe in him to rail against those whose love offends their "sensibilities."
 I had a conversation with a local Boy Scout leader who talked of how gay men turned young boys into being gay. I asked this gentleman if he knew any gay men. He said no. I then asked him where he got such a ridiculous idea. He had no answer, of course- just like the people who write the "I love Jesus and hate gays" letters.
                                                                                   ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  August 27, 1996
School choice only a bogus choice
Editor:
 I see that "school choice" time has rolled around again (notice the Mark Thornhill cartoon of Aug.18). This is one idea that doesn't seem to go away. Ever wonder why the people most enamored with this idea seem to be white Christian types from Orange County with children already enrolled in white, Christian-type private schools? If vouchers ever make it into law, these people suddenly get $2,000 per year, per child to help pay for their children's white, Christian-type education! I can see why they must love the idea. Of course, to sell it to everyone else they must say something like, "Poor people deserve a choice too," or some such drivel- as if $2,000 is going to be enough to pay for a quality private school.
 Where does all this money come from to help pay for their kids' private schooling? From the public schools, of course. I would be ashamed if the California Teacher's Association did not try to fight this proposal.
 Bob Dole made a point of slamming teachers unions during the convention. I say the teachers unions are the collective voice of our hard-working public school teachers, which do the best they can with what they can. I support our teachers and our public schools. Don't be misled on the voucher issue.
 And, if Chris Pulse thinks we shouldn't support the Vista school bond measure (Letters, Aug. 18), that's reason enough right there for me to support it.
 It is kind of pitiful that Pulse is still being such a big baby over the fact that Vista voters threw out his Christian right school trustees.
                                                                                ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  October 4, 1996
Rock 'n' roll an enduring 'demon'
Editor:
 So we have another column on how rock music is destroying our society (Oct.1).
 Richard Kirk seems to have the credentials for this kind of column- teacher of philosophy and religion at The Bishop's School in La Jolla. I am sure he is serious, too. If he had his way, we could all listen to only music that he deems good for us. I seem to recall an old film clip from the days of early rock 'n' roll showing a Southern sheriff talking about how we don't want our daughters listening to "race" music or "jungle rhythms."
 Anyway, it is a free country and Mr. Kirk can postulate whatever he wants. And, being a free country, we need not worry that men like him will ever be able to control our music. To quote a famous poet, "Rock 'n' roll is here to stay, it will never die."
