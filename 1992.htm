Oceanside Blade-Citizen   March 11,1992
Szabo should resign
Editor:
 I'm writing in regard to the March 2 story about public school board members who are for the voucher referendum. Specifically, I was concerned about Oceanside Unified School District Trustee Dean Szabo.
 All these trustees that we have elected are supposed to be looking out for the concerns of public schools. Since when should parents be able to use tax dollars to send their children to Christian schools?
 Mr. Szabo, who sends his kids to a local Christian school, thinks it is unfair that he has to pay double. I gather he is one of those who thinks that if you don't have kids, you shouldn't pay any taxes for public education.
 I think it's unfair that Mr. Szabo has any kind of responsibility in our public schools.
 I call for the immediate resignation of Mr. Szabo and all other trustees who push for religious education.
                                                                                  ERIC PARISH

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   April 21,1992
Not his tax dollars
Editor:
 That was an interesting letter by Mike Lloyd, ("Bring back God," April 10). I was in elementary school in the 1950s and I do recall we all said a prayer in the morning. I also remember we had segregated restrooms and neighborhoods, poll taxes and an absolutely lily-white school. Mr. Lloyd also remarks that schools today teach evolution, sex education and tolerance for others. But they don't teach children that they were created by a God that holds them more valuable than his own son.
 Since I believe in teaching my children the truth, and also because I am not a Christian, I applaud our public schools for exactly the things Mr. Lloyd condemns.
 This ridiculous idea that crime,etc., are somehow caused by our children not saying a prayer to Jesus first thing in the morning makes no sense, and I sure don't want any fundamentalist Christians twisting and warping my child's mind.
 So send your kid to a private Christian school, Mr. Lloyd. But don't use my tax dollars, and keep your blue nose out of the public schools.
                                                                                    ERIC PARISH

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   May 11, 1992
One More Time
Editor:
 If you are going to keep printing more nonsense from Mike Lloyd ("Atheism has no place in our society, May 3), I guess I have to write another letter.
 I suppose I could dismiss this guy as just another ranting religious fanatic. It's just that he keeps talking about evolution as if it was some kind of secular humanist fantasy.
 Evolution of the species is a scientific fact of life, Mr. Lloyd, believe it or not. People who refuse to believe a scientific fact because of their religion I think of as somewhat fanatic.
 It is also rather strange to read letters by Christian fundamentalists who babble about Hitler and Stalin because they were not Christian fundamentalist. Say what?
 Not believing in Jesus Christ as Lord is not a crime, Mr. Lloyd. It also does not make me immoral, full of sin, etc.
 So get another view of life on this planet and quit writing these strange articles in my morning paper.
                                                                               ERIC PARISH

Escondido Times Advocate  August 24,1992
Review of attitudes
 I see that you brought up homosexuality in the Sunday "Question of the Week."
 Considering that the Times Advocate serves Inland North County, I thought the letters were fairly balanced on the whole.
 Of course you will always have the people like Bill Lane - "Gays will keep trying to be like normal people...sexual perversion that it is." "Normal" will always be whatever people like Lane define it to be for the rest of us. I liked Pamela Calvert's linkage of "runaway fathers, homosexuality, and nymphomaniacs" all in one sentence! Amazing!
 And of course you had to get the Christian soldier-us-against-all-evil view from Cherrie Barnett. If sex doesn't result in a dozen kids, it's against the will of God. She also repeats a belief I've heard before from others, that homosexuals "recruit" from our poor "heterosexual" kids. Can anyone actually believe that gay people "recruit" kids into their ranks? Very wierd thinking.
 I'm also rather tired of people like Stephen Beck who have this strange idea that gay people are somehow incapable of having children. It is not that far-fetched at all. Get real. Think about it a minute.
 So I would like to apologize to all my gay and lesbian friends- yes, I have many- who might have opened the Sunday paper and felt slightly left out of American society.
 Things will change for the better, I hope.
                                                                                  Eric Parish

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   September 6, 1992
More on evolution
Editor:
 OK, let's try it again one more time, slowly and clearly.
 Mr. Gordon Hanner (Letters, Aug. 25) admits that the theory of evolution is the philosophical foundation of all "secular" thought, including education, biology, all the life sciences, psychology and the social sciences.
 Then he quotes a Dr. Robert Grange, who says the odds are too high for life to have formed in a primordial ooze, then he says the first and second laws of thermodynamics make evolution impossible.
 The first law is the conservation of mass/energy in a closed system and really has nothing to do with evolution.
 The second law of thermodynamics, the increase in entropy, or disorganization, in a closed system doesn't really apply to life because energy always is being added to living systems, the energy coming ultimately from our sun.
 Mr. Hanner is obviously not a scientist or remotely concerned with discovering the truth of our place in the universe.
 If I didn't know better, I would suspect he is a Christian, trying to persuade the world that all of life was created in three days about 6,000 years ago.
 As such, he has no business using his twisted "facts of science" to say that "evolution is impossible." I must say I am getting tired of this, extremely frustrated, in fact.
 Do some people really decide that they will believe in something, even if all the truth we can see in this world says otherwise?
 I do not understand why some people volunteer to be deaf and blind.
                                                                                        ERIC PARISH
