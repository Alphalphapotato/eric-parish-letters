Oceanside Blade-Citizen   January 21,1994
Letter was strange
Editor:
 I've seen some strange letters in your paper. The one about sterilizing women who apply for AFDC was unbelievable (Herbert Pairitz, Jan. 18).
 Mr. Pairitz, you don't seem to realize that most people receiving AFDC are on for less than two years. It is a temporary cushion for people unable to make ends meet.
 Believe it or not, but men can receive AFDC also. Does your suggestion apply to them, too? Give them vasectomies? While we're at it, let's give a vasectomy to the fathers of all these welfare kids.
 That will show them.
                                                                                 ERIC PARISH
                                                                                              Vista

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   February 19,1994
More on Vista school
Editor:
 I'm glad to see Larry Jones confess to being an "intolerant Christian" (Letters, Feb. 12). I've thought of him that way for some time now.
 I'm not sure I understand what he is intolerant of, however. I didn't realize Vista schools were secretly teaching reckless sex to our public schoolchildren. I've never heard of this before.
 The Blade-Citizen should immediately do a front-page story on this. I'll call up "60 Minutes."
 It also took me a minute to figure out what the "closed doors of theoretical dogmatism" were. I think this means my children won't be taught about Noah's ark in Biology 101.
 I find it hard not to laugh when people start linking "recall" with "crucifixtion." Come now, Mr. Jones. No one is out to crucify anyone. Aren't we getting a little bit carried away here?
 On another subject, I can't understand why the Blade-Citizen is so adamantly opposed to this recall. We are not engaged in a "religious witch hunt." We are only opposed to fundamentalist Christianity being shoved into our public school system.
 I would also oppose Hinduism being taught in the schools.
 Contrary to popular belief, none of these three trustees was elected by a majority vote. Maybe a majority vote can get rid of them.
                                                                               ERIC PARISH
                                                                                             Vista

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   April 15,1994
Trustees should sublimate
Editor:
 I'm sorry to have to mention it, but is anyone else getting kind of tired of C. Antonio Provost and his desire to remove sex from all of our lives (Letters, April 9)? It seems like this running joke has been going on in the Editorial pages forever.
 Provost must write at least 20 letters a year to our North County newspapers talking about how great it is to never have sex, masturbate, etc. I'm glad it works for you, Mr. Provost. I don't think you are going to have much luck with the rest of us, however. You might want to get in touch with the "Moral Majority" on our Vista school board, though. They'll probably sign you up to speak to assemblies of our 11th-grade students.
 Sex seems to be the only thing on the minds of our school board these days anyway. Maybe you could teach trustees Deidre Holliday, John Tyndall and Joyce Lee how to "sublimate," and then maybe we could get on with the business of educating our children.
                                                                                     ERIC PARISH
                                                                                                   Vista

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   July 17,1994
Get rid of Thornhill
Editor:
 I think the North County Blade-Citizen needs a new staff cartoonist. Or at least a little balance. Enough is enough.
 We all know that Mark Thornhill is on the side of the "religious right." He seems to wear it on his sleeve. Who of us can forget the cartoon of the Vista recall people dressed up like the KKK and dancing around a fiery cross? Is this guy slightly paranoid or what?
 I am still curious as to his letter to the editor on Francis Crick, the Nobel Prize winner (June 2). Does Thornhill truly believe he is capable of debating biology and evolution with one of the discoverers of DNA?
 And now he takes on Garry Trudeau, a cartoonist whose insights are on a level far above his own pitiful renderings (June 26).
 The "religious right" can damn homosexuals, atheists, socialists, feminists, liberals, godless secularists and anyone else they desire to hell and back and then claim "religious freedom." Hey, it's the American way.
 If anyone happens to disagree with them, they automatically become "religious bigots."
 How convenient. I'm afraid it doen't work that way, Mr. Thornhill. And I believe the American people can understand that.
                                                                                 ERIC PARISH
                                                                                                Vista

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   December 29, 1994
Protecting rights
Editor:
 It seems like every Christmas we get a present in the letters to the editor from one of our North County "characters." This Christmas it was Robert Garrick of Bonsall, talking about the new class of students and physically sick "criminals" in our midst (Letters, Dec. 25).
 On the anniversary of the birthday of the original "bleeding heart," Garrick takes aim at all of us who still believe in the constitutional safeguards our country was founded upon. A student is not a criminal. Neither is a person waiting in the emergency room of a hospital. Someone standing upon this piece of earth without the necessary "papers" may be a criminal in Garrick's eyes, but then so are 90 percent of the people driving on Highway 78.
 I've noticed that a lot of the support for Proposition 187 comes from people worried about the changing demographics of California. They seem to confuse "illegal immigrants" with Americans who have brown skin and Spanish surnames. Remember the North County white high school kids telling the other high school kids to "go back to Mexico"? Guess where those students learned that. Children in this country need to be in school, period. Sick people should be able to get medical care. We should be able to get beyond all these ethnic differences, especially on Christmas.
                                                                               ERIC PARISH
                                                                                             Vista
