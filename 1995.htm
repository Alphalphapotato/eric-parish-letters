Oceanside Blade-Citizen   March 8, 1995
Balance sought
Editor:
 We must have sinned really badly last week to deserve Chris Pulse and Deidre Holliday both at the same time on your opinion pages (March 1).
 Pulse starts out (as usual) about the public schools indoctrinating our youth with leftist ideology. Before you know it, he is talking about "gansta rap" and "depravity and bestiality"; the phrase "perverts and criminals" shows up twice. Then we have the obligatory "muderers, rapists, child molestors, drug pushers, etc." My son must go to a different public school than Pulse's.
 At least Holliday sticks to her usual anti-abortion tirades.
 We all know where she stands on this. We also know that most of us still desire abortion to be safe and legal.
 Your definition of murder may include abortion; mine does not. I also happen not to agree with your "fixed and non-negotiable moral laws." Am I allowed to call my beliefs "non-negotiable" also?
 A little balance on your editorial pages couldn't hurt.
                                                                                 ERIC PARISH

Escondido Times Advocate  May 8, 1995
The privileges of wealth
Editor:
 I read with interest your editorial ("The lanes toll for thee," April 25) on the opening of the I-15 express lanes to the "high-rollers" who can afford the $100 monthly charge.
 What a great idea! I feel we could use this idea in other areas of public life. For instance, only rich people could buy houses and live in the nicest neighborhoods with the nicest public schools. And people accused of crimes who are wealthy could get out of jail the next day, get the best legal help and the quickest trials.
 We could even take it to extremes. Unless someone was willing to fork over $1,000 or more, they would not be able to get the ear of their elected representatives, like Assemblyman Jan Goldsmith, R-Poway. People with the same interests could band together and pour truckloads of cash into their representatives' pockets so that their problems would be acted upon with the highest priority.
 I even propose a name for these people. How about "lobbyists"?
                                                                                    ERIC PARISH

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   June 3, 1995
A shameful forum
Editor:
 When did God appoint Jim Young as his personal spokesman on what can and cannot coexist with Christianity (Community Forum, May 24)?
 The last time I checked, all the mainstream Protestant denominations and the Roman Catholic Church accepted evolution along with a "symbolic" reading of Genesis. I try to be a tolerant and understanding person, but the attitude of some who accept nothing but a slavishly literal reading of the Bible is beyond my comprehension.
 Are we to return to the Middle Ages with the sun revolving around  a flat earth?
 And then to threaten unbelievers with the coming "wrath" of a God who will send us to hell if we use our minds for thinking? I don't know about your God, but my God doesn't condemn children to eternal damnation just because they've never heard of someone named "Jesus." You should be ashamed.
                                                                                ERIC PARISH

Oceanside Blade-Citizen   July 19, 1995
Who's intolerant?
Editor:
 I noticed that Mike Lloyd has been trying really hard to be tolerant of us "liberal, intolerant tolerance-touters" (Letters, July1). I can understand his position. It would be a boring world if it were full of nothing but self-righteous, sanctimonious people like himself. Everyone would go to heaven and there wouldn't be anyone around to proselytize to or to condemn. It's a good thing God took care of this and made all of us sinners and unbelievers so Lloyd and his ilk will have people to feel holier than.
 By the way, what makes this country great is the "liberal promotion of the right to do wrong." "Wrong" and "illegal"  are two very separate things, and thankfully so.
 I think it says it all about why religion and government don't mix when he calls my God a "made-up God" and his the true "biblical God."
                                                                                ERIC PARISH

Escondido Times Advocate  August 25, 1995
Cruel shot at Garcia
Editor:
 So Morton Posner says good riddance to Garcia (Letters to the Editor,Aug. 21). I do mourn Jerry Garcia. He lived a full life and made great music. I do not believe he is responsible for "thousands of ruined lives." That is simply ludicrous.
 I can say with all honesty that I really do not care one way or another whether you mourn him. Saying "good riddance," however, strikes me as rather cruel.
