Oceanside North County Times  March 7, 1999
Worries about minority status miss real issues
 It seems I can't go more than a week without reading another whining letter lamenting the coming loss of the "white majority". Mr. Ted Kruger's letter (March 1) was especially irritating.
 Will Mr. Kruger's "grandchildren become employed in fast-food chains"? White kids flip burgers nowdays even. "Will we be able to get on welfare and food stamps"? White people are the majority of people collecting welfare right now.  And last but not least, I would like to assure Mr. Kruger that his grandchildren will not be force-fed "guacamole,tacos,refried beans, and Tequila". Even if they are, it sounds tastier than "two eggs over medium and home fries". Too bad Mr. Kruger didn't ask the really interesting questions, like "Will white people be dragged to Africa in chains and become slaves to work on African plantations". Or "Will my grandchildren finally have to learn how to pick their own fruits and vegetables"?
                                                                                ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  July 3, 1999
Where are retractions from the scoffers?
 The stories of mass slaughter and genocide in Kosovo are starting to be backed up with the partially-burnt bones of 15-year old boys, about the age of my son.
 So where are all the letter-writers to this newspaper who scoffed at "NATO's war" and claimed there were no atrocities? Where is the apology for their support of a butcher like Milosevic? Pardon me if I don't hold my breath waiting for the retractions to roll in. On the contrary, soon we will hear stories of the "good Serbs" or the Serbs who were "only following orders". We will probably also hear that all these bones were "planted" by enemies of Yugoslavia.
 There will probably be a few nut cases from Poway who will swear that Clinton and his buddies from the United Nations started the whole war in order to cover up Janet Reno's assassination of David Koresh in Waco, Texas.
 One thing you can count on is that we won't hear from anyone who was actually there when the shots rang out and the bodies were stacked up like cordwood. Once again hate and ignorance seems to win. It is a good thing I subscribe to the view that mankind will someday rise above his animal nature and use his brain for something better than building bigger bombs. Otherwise peoples' ignorance would begin to depress me.
                                                                                   ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  August 3, 1999
Bloated military is outdated
Editor:
 Former Marine Sgt. Philip A. Oien spoke of helicopter noise, military protesters, and the "concept of America" ("Why Protest Those Who Protect Us?",July 26).
 Mr. Oien talks of the monetary value of the military to our economy. I assume he means all the big bucks that the federal government spends on the military these days, $300 billion nationwide. I find it hard to believe that most of the money spent training our bloated military couldn't have just as easily been flushed down a toilet. Or that we really need $200 million dollar fighter jets. Or that we need our spending on "defense" to be more than the rest of the world spends combined.
 I also find it odd that all our "pro-military" congressmen who ladle wheelbarrows of military cash into our districts are the same ones who want "government off our backs" and think social welfare destroys poor people. I can think of much, much better things to do with federal tax dollars. Especially now that there is no real military threat to our country.
 We will most likely never again see any lowering of the war budget or any kind of a "peace dividend". We won't have the money for universal medical insurance. We won't have the money for our schools to be number one. We won't be able to help lift the third world into peace and prosperity.
 
                                                                                   ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times  August 31, 1999
Thank goodness it's not his job to decide
  The U.S. Supreme Court has and most likely will continue to rule that women have the right to abortion for any reason when the fetus is unable to live on its own outside the womb. This is agreed to be the first two trimesters of a nine-month pregnancy.
 The Supreme Court has and will continue to rule that any laws regulating that third trimester must take into consideration the health of the mother, physical and mental. This is why Mr. Hans Neumann ("Dancing on Tiny Graves",August 25) and the others who have pounced upon "partial-birth" abortions as a way to begin limiting a woman's right to choose will change the minds of no one.
 Mr. Neumann may be emotionally hard as a rock when it comes to giving birth to a child with an open spinal column and a few painful months to live, or with any of the other countless cruelties nature can impose on newborn flesh. Mr. Neumann is not a mother, however. And it is not his job to decide on how much physical and mental pain a woman should go through in order to satisfy his own beliefs on abortion. Thank goodness.
 
                                                                                     ERIC PARISH

Oceanside North County Times October 12, 1999
Media doesn't cause problems, it reflects them
Editor:
 Richard Kirk is at it again, fuming about the cultural elites who are destroying the morals of our children ("Parents are undermined, then blamed."October 5).
 I was trying to follow his line of reasoning  that somehow the makers of mass entertainment are lessening the influence parents have on their children. I could see how this might be a subject for further study, maybe by a sociology professional.
Then all of a sudden he was comparing my favorite TV show, "South Park", to pedophiles and drug dealers and ranting about "toxic cultural images" and the recent tragic shootings at high schools. That's when Mr. Kirk lost me. I don't see the media causing our problems. They only reflect where our society wants to be.
 And regarding "that time not long ago" when our culture helped raise moral offspring: Are we talking about that time in the '50s when only white males had morals? Or that time when we were morally napalming Vietnamese? Or even earlier, when our ancestors morally wiped out most American Indians?
 I think watching more "South Park" would do Mr. Kirk some good. It might loosen him up a little.
                                                                                     ERIC PARISH
